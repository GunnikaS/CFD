\section{Introduction}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The flow past bluff bodies is a canonical problem in fluid mechanics, with applications ranging from structural engineering to heat exchanger design. While circular and square cylinders have been extensively studied, triangular cylinders offer an interesting geometric variant due to their asymmetric wake behavior that depends strongly on orientation relative to the incoming flow.

In this study, we simulate incompressible laminar flow past a triangular cylinder in two orientations: one iteration with the vertex facing upstream and the second iteration with the flat face facing upstream. Using the open-source CFD solver OpenFOAM~7 with the \texttt{icoFoam} solver, we investigate how orientation affects the drag coefficient $C_d$, lift coefficient $C_l$, vortex shedding frequency (Strouhal number $St$), and wake structure across a range of Reynolds numbers. Results are validated against the published benchmark data of Kumar De and Dalal~\cite{kumardalal2006}.

Because extensive benchmark data exist for circular and square cylinders, and targeted data exists for triangular cylinders, this study provides an opportunity to gain confidence in our numerical predictions through direct comparison with published results, while also examining the effect of bluff body geometry on the key flow quantities.
